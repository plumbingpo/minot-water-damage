In Minot, severe weather such as flash flooding, tropical systems, hurricanes and hard rain can leave a property badly water damaged. Wind-driven rain pushes water in through damaged roofing, broken windows and loose siding. A roof leak can soak insulation, ceilings and the cavities inside your walls. Lower levels flood when storm drains are overwhelmed by a sudden surge of water. Minot Restoration provides emergency storm response: we stop more water from getting in, pump out what is standing and begin restoring the property immediately.

We handle every stage of weather-related water damage: tarping and boarding up to stop further intrusion, extracting the water, drying the structure and rebuilding damaged components. We know a storm rarely leaves just one problem, because water entering through roof damage harms walls and ceilings and then feeds mold when drying is not done right. We address all of these connected issues under one coordinated restoration plan.

After storm damage your insurer will want records, and we supply them: photos, moisture readings, written damage assessments and a detailed scope of work. Throughout the claim, our team deals with your insurer directly and can speak up on your behalf so storm-related damage is assessed fairly.

What to Expect From Our Storm Damage Repair

We follow a proven process to ensure every job is completed to the highest standards. Here's how we handle storm damage repair from start to finish:

1

Emergency Board-Up & Tarping

Our crew covers damaged roofing, boards up shattered windows and seals openings, which keeps additional water out until permanent repairs can be planned.

2

Extract Water and Assess Damage

Our team pumps and vacuums out the water that came in with the storm. Thermal cameras and moisture meters let us inspect every affected area and chart how far the damage reaches.

3

Drying the Structure

Commercial dehumidifiers and air movers dry every affected structural component, including roof decking, insulation, wall cavities and flooring, to prevent mold growth.

4

Permanent Repairs & Reconstruction

We repair or replace the flooring, insulation, drywall, siding, roofing and anything else the storm damaged. The goal is a property back in the shape it was in before the storm.

Maintenance Tips for Storm Damage Repair

A little prevention goes a long way in avoiding expensive restoration emergencies. Here are our expert recommendations:

  • Trim trees near your home to lower the risk of wind-driven branch damage
  • Clogged gutters overflow in a downpour, so clear them and the downspouts often
  • Take dated photos of the house every year, since insurers ask about prior condition
  • Have your roof checked annually and fix vulnerable areas ahead of the stormy months
  • In areas that see hurricanes, fit storm shutters or windows rated for impact
  • Runoff should flow away from your foundation, so fix low spots and add downspout extensions

Why Hire a Professional for Storm Damage Repair?

The repairs a storm leaves behind are tangled together, and sorting them out correctly calls for an experienced restoration crew. What seems like a small leak indoors can hide widespread water damage in wall cavities, roof decking and insulation, which turns into mold and structural decay unless it is identified and dried properly. Our technicians trace where the water traveled using thermal imaging plus moisture meters, so no affected area is overlooked.

Tarping and boarding up only work when they are done with the right technique. An improperly installed tarp can create new problems by channeling water or failing under wind load. Experienced in storm mitigation, our team picks appropriate methods and materials to keep the building protected until permanent repair work can be scheduled.

Signs It Is Time for Storm Damage Repair

Catching these issues early helps you avoid costly problems and protect your property. Here are the most common indicators that it's time to call a professional:

  • Wind-driven rain coming in through windows, doors or wall penetrations
  • Puddles near skylights, beside a chimney or on the attic floor
  • Rooms that start to smell stale or moldy following a storm
  • New brown rings or damp marks on walls and ceilings once the storm passes
  • A basement or ground floor that floods when the drains cannot keep up
  • Metal flashing pulled away from vents, valleys or the edge of the roof
  • Bubbling paint and soft, drooping spots overhead once the rain stops
